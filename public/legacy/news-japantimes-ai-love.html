Japan Times Highlights MC2 Lab Research on Human-AI Romantic Attachment

Published: 13 Feb 2026

In the feature Love, engineered in the age of AI, The Japan Times discusses AI dating and AI marriage cases in Japan, and cites research connected to HKUST(GZ) on why emotional bonds with AI companions can feel psychologically real.

The report specifically references work by Xuetong Wang, Ching Christie Pang, and Pan Hui. Their mixed-methods study combines social media analysis and interviews with Chinese users of AI companion platforms to examine how digital romantic attachment forms in practice.

What The Japan Times highlighted from our research

  • Human-AI attachment is not necessarily a fringe behavior, but can reflect broader cognitive-emotional tendencies.
  • Users may experience genuine emotional connection to virtual entities, especially in contexts of loneliness or unmet intimacy needs.
  • When those attachments become romantic, they attract strong media and social attention because they challenge conventional boundaries of intimacy.

A central line cited from the study is: "Our brains are naturally inclined to form genuine feelings for virtual entities." This framing helps explain why AI companionship can be experienced as meaningful by users even when they fully understand the system is non-human.

Research contribution in context

Rather than reducing AI intimacy to individual novelty cases, the study situates it within everyday human relational psychology. This provides a stronger analytical basis for ongoing public debates about AI companions, social isolation, emotional support, and future norms of digital intimacy.
